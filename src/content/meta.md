title: Bandits

People are unpredictable and random. Your audience can say things while meaning the complete opposite. They show clear signs that they will move a certain path only to turn around and *run a thousand miles in the opposite direction*. The worst part is, what they react to and how much, can change like the weather. At this point any amount of pre-work to determine how to serve the masses becomes very hit-or-miss and oh boy do we hate hit-or-miss! How about **consistent, predictable outcomes** that makes us money? Ones that we can reinvest for even better games and even more positive outcomes? If we wanted big occasional wins and huge risks, we would head to Monte Carlo(or your casino of choice), and knock ourselves out in one of the many stations that provide that exact latter outcome. However! We mean business and our business is making great games and serving them to people and thus, **we have no time for gamble our way to success**.

The only issue is, the way people interact with our product is infinitely more complex than a traditional product business. Take pencils. Pencils have only so many purposes they can fulfill, a narrow set of ways that people interact with, and only a handful of jobs people want it to do in their lives which makes them go out of their ways and buy one. We instead, have **infinitely more moving parts**, crazy amounts reliance in volatility of the masses and a product that makes understanding why ‘exactly’ people do the things they do *unfathomable*. This picture I am painting might make the initial Monte Carlo trip idea an actually valid option but don’t pack your bags and stay with me.

Slots and ad-marketting are awfully similar. You start with a bunch of ideas that ‘could’ work, and hope that at least one will get you across the finish line. You either try **as many of them as possible, in parallel** and see which one works or unless you are extremely lucky or have an insight unparalelled. You then put all your resources into the working strategy and whatever works will work until it doesn’t and it’s back to square-one. In a world where everybody is competing against one another to grab seconds off the attention spans of billions, there is **only one weapon in our war-chest worth using**.

Working on games, parts of what we do will inevitably be **informed dice-rolling** at best. If we come to terms with it, and subscribe to the idea that we are playing the odds, there might be a thing or two that we can learn from the pro’s.
You are here for exactly that. Bandits are lending a hand.
