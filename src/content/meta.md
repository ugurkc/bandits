eyebrow: An interactive essay on decisions under uncertainty
title: Bandit
---

People are very, very unpredictable — they say things meaning the complete opposite, they show clear signs that they will want something and then turn around and run a thousand miles in the opposite direction, and any amount of pre-work to determine how to serve the masses has a coin-flip's chance of making you feel stupid for even attempting such blasphemy. If you've ever run ads for your game, or tested a few features simultaneously to see what people resonate with, you know exactly what I am talking about: you start with a bunch of ideas that "could" work and hope that one will get you across the finish line, you run as many of them as possible simultaneously and see which one works, and whatever works sets the standard you try to beat next. In a world where everybody is competing against one another to grab seconds off the attention span of billions, there is only one way to success — the Ol' Reliable, trial and error. I might have painted the picture of a bunch of us Ocean's Eleven'ing into Monte Carlo, but it turns out that to understand the high-stakes problems we face, gamblers might lend a hand — so next time you have to sit in front of a bunch of bandit-like systems and pull results out of an A/B test, you will have a few strategies up your sleeve for when you don't have the time or the money to find out what works the slow way.
