The race above is a clean version of the problem: one pull, one outcome, pick the next arm. Act two makes it messier and more honest by adding a shared budget — instead of choosing one campaign to run, you decide how many dollars go to each of the three every week, which means every allocation is really three simultaneous, differently-sized bets rather than one clean pull. Spread the money evenly and you learn a little about everything but nothing decisively; put it all on your current favorite and you learn a lot about one arm while staying blind to the other two. That is the same explore-exploit trade-off from the first section, just recast as a spending decision instead of a single choice — which is closer to what an actual marketing budget looks like than any tidy row of slot machines.
